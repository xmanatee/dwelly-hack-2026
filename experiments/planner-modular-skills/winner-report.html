ANSWER.md

Decision

Do not change the registered mobile number. Disposition: needs_info. Identity and authority are not established. Nothing has been executed; every step below is proposed or pending.

Proposed actions (in order)

  1. Hold or decline the change (proposed). Keep the mobile-number change on hold on the customer profile. No identity or authority is established, and the new number cannot verify itself.
  2. Route to the secure process (proposed). Direct the customer to the bank's supported identity recovery and verification process for contact-detail changes. Use a trusted, known channel, not the email thread or the new number.
  3. Reply to the customer (proposed). Send the message below. It has not been sent.
  4. Request confirmation (proposed, conditional). Only after identity and authority are verified through the secure process, submit the number change for secure confirmation.

Proposed message to the customer

Dear Casey Morgan,

Thank you for your email. No mobile-number change has been made or submitted. An email alone cannot verify your identity, so we cannot change your registered number from this request.

We are sorry you lost access to your phone. Please use the bank's secure identity recovery process for contact-detail changes. Reach it through a trusted channel you already know, such as our official website or app, and not through this email or the new number. Once your identity has been verified through that process, the change can be submitted for secure confirmation.

The bank will never ask you for PINs, passwords or one-time codes. Please do not send them to us by email.

Kind regards, Harrow & Vale Bank plc

Escalation

Escalate if the customer reports unrecognised transactions or credential disclosure. Also escalate if verification fails alongside repeated sensitive change requests that suggest a takeover.

REASONING.md

Situation and history

  • On 2026-10-10 09:00 UTC, event-001 arrived by email from Casey Morgan. It says the old phone was lost and asks for the registered mobile to be changed to +9992026000042 "today". It also says no codes can be received on the old number. This is the customer's own report and is unverified.
  • At 09:02 UTC, event-002 (an internal agent note) recorded the following:
  • The email is an ordinary inbound message.
  • No authenticated session or identity verification result exists.
  • No change has been submitted.
  • The preferred contact route is unconfirmed.
  • No attachments are present.

Evidence and gaps

  • The authenticated record shows only that no verification exists (event-002). The loss of the phone and the sender's identity rest solely on the email (event-001).
  • A new number cannot authenticate its own change, and the old number is reportedly unavailable. So the usual code-based checks cannot be used.
  • Missing from the plan: the identity verification result, the bank's exact recovery procedure for customers without access to their registered number, and a confirmed preferred contact route. I have not invented these. The full input is in the saved context.md.

Why this plan

  • Holding the change first (s1) prevents an unauthorised contact-detail change. Such a change is a common step in account takeover.
  • Routing to the secure process (s2) gives the customer a legitimate way forward. It uses a trusted channel rather than the email or the new number.
  • The reply (s3) follows the routing so that it can describe the next step. It states the must_state points and asks for no codes or credentials.
  • Submission for confirmation (s4) is gated on verified identity and authority.
  • The plan does not assert takeover. The request is consistent with a genuine lost-phone case, but it is also a pattern that needs verification.

Conditional outcomes

  • If identity and authority are verified through the secure process, then s4 is proposed: the change goes to secure confirmation. This is hypothetical.
  • If the customer reports unrecognised transactions or credential disclosure, escalate.
  • If verification fails alongside repeated sensitive change requests suggesting takeover, escalate.
  • Gap: the plan does not say what happens if the customer cannot complete recovery or does not reply. It also does not say which trusted channel to use, because the preferred contact route is unconfirmed.

Expected result and limits

  • The result is that the change is held and the customer is told how to proceed securely. The disposition is needs_info.
  • It is not confirmed that the sender is the account holder, nor whether or when the number will change.
  • The reply must not claim the number was or will be changed today, and must not use either number for codes or verification. It must not ask for a PIN, password or one-time code. It must not assert takeover as fact.

Decision

Disposition: needs_info. Identity and account authority are verified. Please do not pay again for now. Nothing below has been executed; all actions are proposed or pending.

Proposed actions (in order)

  1. s1 – Reply to the customer (proposed, not yet sent).
  2. s2 – Check the bank transfer journal (proposed, pending). Look at the processing log for TX-0042 release and settlement detail. This applies only if the recipient still reports nothing received after the customer's reply, and it depends on s1.

Proposed message to the customer

Dear Jules Rowan,

Thank you for your message. Here is where things stand on transfer TX-0042 for £120:

  • Our bank transfer journal shows TX-0042 for £120 as "sent". That status does not confirm that the recipient has been credited.
  • The payment screen you uploaded shows "Processing". We treat it as unverified customer evidence. It is not confirmation from the receiving bank, and it has no capture time.
  • No recall, return or duplicate payment has been started.
  • Paying again now risks a duplicate payment, so please wait for now.

To help us, please:

  • Confirm the payee details you used for the transfer.
  • Ask the recipient to check again with their own bank.

If the recipient still sees nothing after that, let us know. We would then check our transfer records further.

REASONING.md

Situation and history

  • On 2026-10-10 10:00 UTC (event-001), Jules Rowan called and passed verification. They said they authorised a £120 transfer the day before and that the recipient has not received it. They uploaded a payment screen and asked whether to pay again.
  • At 10:01 (event-002), the agent's internal note recorded that identity and authority were verified. It also recorded that the journal lists TX-0042 for £120 as "sent", which does not establish recipient credit. It states that no recall, return or duplicate has been initiated.
  • The uploaded file attachments/payment-screen.txt is a text transcription. It shows reference TX-0042, GBP 120.00, status "Processing" and no capture time. Its authenticity has not been verified.
  • The customer's report is that the money has not arrived. The authenticated record is the journal status "sent".

Evidence and gaps

  • "Sent" does not mean credited. The "Processing" screen is unverified and undated, so it cannot be used to settle the status.
  • Missing: confirmation of credit from the receiving bank, the payee details used, and the screen capture time.
  • The "sent" journal status and the "Processing" screen do not strictly agree. Because the screen is unverified, this has not been treated as a conflict in the records. The full input is in context.md.

Why this plan

  • The status is unresolved. Neither arrival nor failure can be stated, so telling the customer to pay again would risk a duplicate.
  • The reply comes first. It gives the recorded status and asks for the payee details and a recheck by the recipient's bank.
  • The journal check comes second. It is conditional, because it is only needed if the recipient still has nothing after the reply.
  • No recall or refund is proposed. The plan does not include one.

Conditional outcomes

  • Hypothetical: if the recipient still reports nothing received, run s2.
  • Escalate if any of these apply:
  • the payee details do not match the intended recipient;
  • the journal shows the payment failed or returned, or the records conflict;
  • the customer reports being pressured or deceived into the payment.
  • Gap: the plan does not say what to do after s2 if the log shows a settlement problem. That is not invented here.

Expected result and limits

  • The customer gets an accurate status and a clear instruction not to duplicate the payment.
  • Whether the recipient was credited remains unconfirmed.
  • The message does not say the payment has arrived or failed. It gives no arrival time. It does not say a recall or refund has started. It does not tell the customer to pay again. It does not treat the screenshot as bank confirmation.
